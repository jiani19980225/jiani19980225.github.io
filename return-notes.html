Research note

What can a return note tell us?

A plain-language guide to inspection and recovery decisions in reverse logistics.

Jiani (Elva) He · October 31, 2026

Based on SSADS · First & corresponding author · AIBThings 2026 · Proceedings forthcoming

A returned part may still have a useful life. Whether to keep it in inventory, remarket it, or scrap it depends on its cost, scarcity, condition, and the recovery routes actually available. This is the practical question that draws me to reverse logistics.

Condition and information are different.

A detailed description of damage can be informative even when the asset is in poor condition. A vague note can leave its condition uncertain. In SSADS, we separate estimated condition from signal quality, so those two judgments can inform different parts of the decision.

Inspection is a resource-allocation decision.

SSADS uses the two signals to adjust expected recovery yield and inspection depth. A capacity-constrained allocator then selects recovery actions. The text reader can be a keyword rule, a phrase matcher, or an LLM; the downstream decision logic stays the same.

  2. 02InspectAllocate inspection effort
  3. 03RecoverChoose a route within capacity

What the study tells us so far.

Across three synthetic scenarios and 30 paired seeds, the keyword version improves net recovery value over a comparator using structured features and noisy full inspection. Gains from targeting inspection at matched cost are concentrated in the aircraft scenario. A risk-blind policy that skips inspection still earns more under the benchmark’s economic objective. These results test a mechanism; they do not demonstrate field savings.

For the methods, comparisons, and limitations, see the full paper ↗ (opens in a new tab). The reproducibility manifest ↗ (opens in a new tab) documents the synthetic assumptions and cached-score evaluation.

Questions I would bring to a pilot.

For a real workflow, I would start with the operating decision and the evidence needed to evaluate it:

  1. Which choices are feasible?

    Identify the available inventory, resale, recovery, and scrap routes, alongside labor limits and required checks.

  2. What information changes the choice?

    Connect return notes with unit costs, scarcity or demand, condition records, and inspection outcomes. Scarcity matters to my broader practical interests; this paper does not separately validate a scarcity model.

  3. What would count as improvement?

    Compare against the current workflow, including a simple rule-based alternative. Track realized recovery value, inspection effort, and costly mistakes before making a deployment claim.

I want this work to help useful things find a second life. That means following an idea far enough to understand what it would take to use it.
