01Research

A second life for useful hardware.

Many returned parts still have value. My main research interest is AI-assisted reverse logistics: how to use information about an asset to decide what needs inspection and what should return to inventory, be remarketed, or be scrapped. My industry work is in data center supply chains.

The SSADS study uses three synthetic reverse-logistics scenarios. Related papers examine retail forecasting, simulated supply-chain interventions, and LLM-routing assurance.

02A closer look

First & corresponding author · AIBThings 2026

Which returned assets deserve an inspection?

Returned equipment often arrives with a note and an unknown condition. In SSADS, text readers—including an LLM—turn those notes into condition and signal-quality estimates. Inspection and recovery logic use that evidence under a shared labor budget.

  1. 01Read the noteCondition + signal quality
  2. 02Allocate inspectionShared labor budget
  3. 03Choose recoveryExpected value + capacity

My contribution

I proposed the core research idea and methodological framework for SSADS: using semantic signals from return notes to inform inspection and recovery allocation.

What we found

In three synthetic scenarios, the keyword implementation improved recovery value over a noisy full-inspection comparator while reducing inspection cost. At equal inspection cost, targeting added value in the aircraft scenario and had little economic effect in the other two settings.

Scope & limits of the evaluation

These are benchmark results, not measured savings from a production deployment. A risk-blind policy that skipped inspection scored higher under the benchmark’s purely economic objective.

03Research directions

The questions connecting the papers.

Direction 01 · Reverse logistics

A second life for returned hardware

In SSADS, we treat return notes as imperfect evidence for deciding how much inspection to allocate before choosing a recovery route.

The question
Returned assets arrive with uncertain condition and free-text notes. Choosing how deeply to inspect each one, and whether it goes to reuse, resale, recycling, or disposal, is a resource-allocation problem under partial information.
Industry context
Tracking reverse-logistics cycle times and guiding disposition decisions for retired data center hardware
Research setting
Three synthetic reverse-logistics scenarios: IT decommissioning, aircraft maintenance, and consumer-electronics returns.
Related papers
  • Inspection and recovery from return notesFirst author · Accepted · AIBThings 2026

Direction 02 · Decision support & trustworthy AI

Which action is worth taking?

In DACRI, we compare interventions under explicit costs, delays, and incomplete information. Related work examines the policy and provenance evidence needed to qualify an LLM-routing system for deployment.

The question
Detecting a disruption is not the same as knowing which intervention recovers the most value. Good decisions need causal evidence and explicit costs and delays. AI-enabled decisions also need provenance that people can review before deployment.
Industry context
Rebalancing inventory across sites and standardizing enterprise LLM workflows
Research setting
Controlled supply-chain simulations with causal ground truth; a separate study of deployment assurance for LLM request routing.
Related papers
  • DACRI: ranking supply-chain interventionsCo-first author · Accepted · ICECCME 2026
  • Evidence for LLM-routing deploymentAccepted · ICOAI 2026

Direction 03 · Forecasting & planning

Forecasts planners can act on

With collaborators, I study the trade-off between forecast accuracy and stability, early warning for delivery risk, and the operational value of added planning capabilities.

The question
A forecast can score well on point accuracy and still move sharply between adjacent time steps. A planning tool also needs to be evaluated through the cost and feasibility of the plans it produces.
Industry context
Analyzing demand, stock targets, and shortages across a data center supply network
Research setting
Retail-demand forecasting experiments; forecast-to-plan replay on Favorita, M5, and Walmart data; separate work on late-delivery risk.
Related papers
