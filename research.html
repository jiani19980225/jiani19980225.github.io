01Research program

Evidence for Operational Decisions

When does better evidence lead to better decisions?

My research begins with questions that keep recurring in operations: forecasts that are accurate but unstable, disruptions that are detected but not resolved, and returned assets whose condition is uncertain.

I don’t judge models only by their predictive accuracy. I evaluate them by the decisions they change. That means paying attention to constraints, costs, delays, partial information, and whether people can trust and review the evidence behind an action.

02Why this work matters

Decisions that keep critical systems running.

Supply chains and AI systems now sit underneath much of the economy. When a forecast swings without reason, a disruption is met with the wrong intervention, or an AI recommendation cannot be traced, the cost shows up as shortages, idle capacity, discarded hardware, and lost trust. My research asks how to make these decisions measurably better, and how to show that they are.

Supply-chain resilience

Acting on disruptions, not just detecting them

Disruptions in critical supply chains ripple into manufacturing, health care, and national security. Planners need to know which intervention recovers the most value when information is partial and delayed, and they need forecasts steady enough to plan around. DACRI ranks interventions by the net value they recover; my forecasting and late-delivery work aims to give planners earlier, steadier signals.

Related workDACRI: decision-aware causal intervention rankingAccuracy-preserving stability regularization for forecastingLate-delivery risk prediction and early warning

Trustworthy AI

Evidence that AI improves real decisions

As organizations bring AI into operational decisions, they need evidence that a new capability actually changes outcomes, and that its recommendations can be traced, reviewed, and governed before deployment. With collaborators, I have proposed an observable evidence standard for planning capabilities and a provenance-aware assurance framework for routing requests to large language models.

Related workObservable evidence standard for planning capabilitiesDeployment assurance for LLM request routing

Recovering value

A second life for returned technology

Returned and retired electronics must be inspected and routed to reuse, resale, recycling, or disposal, usually with limited inspection labor. Better routing keeps usable hardware in service and recovers valuable materials instead of discarding them. My first-author paper shows how semantic signals in free-text return notes can direct inspection effort where it matters most.

03Research streams

Three connected questions.

Each stream starts from a problem met in practice and asks what evidence a better decision needs.

Question 01

When do better predictions actually produce better plans?

Forecasting & Planning Value

Motivation
A more accurate forecast is not automatically a better planning input. Forecasts that swing from one run to the next create churn in replenishment, and a new analytical capability creates value only if it changes the plans that are executed.
Origin in practice
Analyzing demand, stock targets, and shortages for a technology client’s supply network
Representative problems
Forecast stabilityForecast-to-plan replayLate-delivery riskEarly warningPlanning value attribution

Question 02

Which action should come next, and when can the evidence behind it be trusted?

Causal Decision Support & Trustworthy AI

Motivation
Detecting a disruption is not the same as knowing which intervention recovers the most value. Good decisions need causal evidence and explicit costs and delays. AI-enabled decisions also need provenance that people can review before deployment.
Origin in practice
Rebalancing inventory across sites and standardizing enterprise LLM workflows

How can unstructured signals guide inspection and recovery when labor is scarce?

AI-Assisted Reverse Logistics

Motivation
Returned assets arrive with uncertain condition and free-text notes. Choosing how deeply to inspect each one, and whether it goes to reuse, resale, recycling, or disposal, is a resource-allocation problem under partial information.
Origin in practice
Tracking reverse-logistics cycle times and guiding asset-disposition decisions
