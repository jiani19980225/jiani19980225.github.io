01Philosophy

What I believe.

Some of the most useful things I know, I learned from operations. Here are a few stories and principles that shape how I work and live.

02Life stories

What shapes me.

Why I chose optimization

I have always believed that the world has enough resources. What it often lacks is the ability to use them well and get them to the right place. That belief is why I chose to study operations research and optimization: at heart, they are the science of allocating what we have so that it helps more people.

I see the same pattern in my work today: inventory sitting at one site while another runs short, returned devices that could still be used, inspection hours spent where they matter least. Each one is a chance to do more with what already exists, and that is the kind of problem I want to spend my career on.

Always learning

I am someone who always wants to keep improving. Since graduating, I have kept learning well beyond my field, from investing to a range of sports, because I want to grow as a whole person and not only as a professional.

I hope to keep growing in every direction for the rest of my life.

Someone people can talk to

I think of myself as an energetic, warm person, and the people around me often come to me when they are facing a problem. Many times, after we talk, I see them leave a little lighter, and nothing makes me happier than that.

Whether I am mentoring a junior analyst or talking through a problem with a friend, being able to help is one of the things that makes my life feel meaningful.

03Principles

What I try to live by.

  1. 01

    Judge a model by the decision it changes.

    It is easy to fall in love with a clever method. Before I commit to a research question, I ask: if this works completely, will a real decision get better? If not, I keep looking for a better question.

  2. 02

    Say exactly what you know.

    Evidence earns trust only when it is reported honestly: what was tested, what wasn’t, and how sure we are. I would rather make a smaller claim that holds than a bigger one that doesn’t.

  3. 03

    Everything deserves a second life.

    In reverse logistics, a returned device isn’t waste; it’s an asset whose next use hasn’t been decided yet. I try to treat setbacks the same way: inspect them honestly, keep what’s valuable, and route the rest toward something new.

  4. 04

    Steady beats spectacular.

    My forecasting research favors predictions that don’t swing wildly from week to week, because steadiness is what lets others plan. I try to be that kind of colleague and friend: consistent, reliable, and there when it counts.

The world has always had enough resources. The work is in using them well, and that is how I hope to help more people.
